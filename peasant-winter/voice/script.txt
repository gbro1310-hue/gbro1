England, thirteen hundred. It's November, and tonight you sleep with the cow.

Not near the cow. With her. One roof, one room. People at this end, animals at that end, and a drain down the middle.

This is not poverty. This is heating. A grown cow gives off as much heat as a small modern heater. All night. For free.

The fire sits in the middle of the floor. There is no chimney. The smoke leaves through the thatch, and through your eyes.

Before you sleep, you bury the embers in ash, so they last until morning.

In towns, a bell told you when. Couvre-feu. Cover the fire. Curfew.

Not every animal makes it to Christmas. There isn't enough hay. The old English name for November was blood month.

So you lie down on straw, beside your family, beside the cow, and listen to her breathe.

Not small. Not sad. Just warm enough to reach the morning.
