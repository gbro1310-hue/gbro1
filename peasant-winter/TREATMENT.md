# Tonight You Sleep With the Cow — treatment

Style: Woodcut Print (`woodcut`). Channel: Ordinary Lives (2nd-person immersive history).
Format: YouTube Short, 1080×1920, 24 fps, ~58 s, English voice-over (channel ElevenLabs voice), letterpress captions in the bottom paper margin.

## 1. Three candidate structures
1. **A night in five plates (book of hours).** Dusk, hearth, curfew, slaughter-month, sleep: one locked plate each. Calm, readable, but static for a Short's first 3 s.
2. **The same block recut.** One plate of the longhouse is carved deeper as the night goes on: first the outline, then the cow, then the fire, then the sleepers; the colour plate (fire) spreads and finally withdraws to a single ember. Strong continuity, one "world".
3. **Two blocks printed in turn.** Human end / animal end alternate impressions until one sheet carries both. Clever, but the "same room" idea is clearer when both are in one frame from the start.

**Chosen: 2 + one close-up and one mirrored-word plate.** Open on a snap close-up of the cow's eye (hook), pull back into ONE longhouse plate that keeps being recut; break out only twice: the curfew bell plate (mirrored → true print, the native move) and the November hard-cut series. End back on the main plate, inked down to black with one ember left (an "uncarved corner" ending).

## 2. Logline and arc
Logline: In a medieval longhouse, the warmest thing in your bedroom is a cow, and the most important job of the night is keeping one ember alive.
Setup: you share one room with the animals. Turn: the fire must be covered for the night (curfew) and the herd must shrink before winter. Ending: you lie in the dark listening to the cow breathe; one ember survives.

## 3. Benchmark
Lynd Ward-style wordless woodcut novels (sequence of self-contained plates) and Käthe Kollwitz woodcuts (emotion in gouge direction).
Learn: plate-by-plate pacing, white figures out of black wood, light only where cut. Don't take: figures, borders, compositions, titles.

## 6. Cue map
72 bpm (0.833 s/beat). Drone (hurdy-gurdy-like, D) from 3.6 s; log drum on cuts; plucked low strings (synth) ticking under 12.6–27; near-silence 33.8–35.0 before the bell; bell strike (modal, inharmonic) at 35.0 = loudest; November series: one log-drum hit per hard cut; final section: drone only, dying with the ember; true silence on the last 0.6 s.

## 8. Type
Captions: letterpress serif in the bottom paper margin, ink, never over the plate; held ≥ max(1.8 s, line + 0.6 s). Carved words (COUVRE-FEU / CURFEW / BLOOD MONTH) cut into the block. One colour plate: burnt copper-orange, only for fire and embers. No voice/subtitle omissions.
