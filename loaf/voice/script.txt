London, the early thirteen hundreds. You are a baker's boy, and the loaf in your hands is a little too light.

Not by much. Nobody would notice.

Somebody will. This city weighs its bread. The price of grain sets how heavy a penny loaf must be, and men come to the shop to check.

Here is what happens to a baker whose bread comes up short. The first time, he is tied to a hurdle and dragged through the dirtiest streets, where the most people are. The light loaf hangs from his neck.

The second time, the pillory. One hour, in front of everyone who buys from him.

The third time, they pull down his oven. He never bakes in this city again.

So you put the loaf back on the scale. You add dough.

That is what an ordinary life is. Not small. Just weighed, every single day.
