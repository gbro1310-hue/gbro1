**Title:** What Happened to Medieval Bakers Who Sold Light Bread #historyshorts #dailyhistory

**Description:**

London, the early 1300s. The penny loaf had a legal weight, set by the price of grain (the Assize of Bread), and officers came into the shop to check it. A baker caught selling short bread was dragged through the streets on a hurdle with the light loaf hung from his neck; a second time meant the pillory; a third, his oven pulled down and the trade forbidden to him in the city.

Sources: the Assize of Bread and Ale (13th c.); the Liber Albus of the City of London (compiled 1419, from older customs); H. T. Riley, Memorials of London and London Life (1868).
