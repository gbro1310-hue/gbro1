Pompeii, 79 AD. You hand your dirty toga to the fuller. Here's what happens next.

First, he drops it into a tub of stale urine. Human and animal. Collected from jars left out on street corners.

The ammonia eats the grease and whitens the wool.

Then a worker climbs in, barefoot, and stomps on your clothes. For hours. That's the job. All day. Ankle-deep in pee.

Rinse. Rub in fuller's earth, a clay that soaks up the last of the oil. Brush it. Bleach it over burning sulphur. Press it flat.

And the urine? It was so valuable, Emperor Vespasian put a tax on it. When his son complained, he held a coin to his nose and asked if it smelled.

It didn't.

Your toga comes back gleaming white. It just took a lot of strangers' pee to get there.
