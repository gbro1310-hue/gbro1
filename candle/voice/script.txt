Paris, thirteen oh three. It is getting dark, and you are twelve years old.

In front of you is a piece of silk with gold thread in it, half finished. On the table there is a candle. You are not allowed to light it.

That is not your master being cheap. It is the law. This year the embroiderers of Paris wrote it into their rules: no work by candlelight.

Here is the reason they give. Work done by candlelight comes out worse than work done by day. A candle is yellow, and it moves. A crooked stitch can look straight.

So when the light goes, the needle goes down. In June, that is a long day. In December, it is a short one.

You will do this for eight years.

That is what an ordinary life is. Not small. Just made, very carefully, in daylight.
