**Title:** Why Medieval Apprentices Couldn't Light a Candle #historyshorts #dailyhistory

**Description:**

Paris, 1303. It's getting dark, the gold embroidery is half finished — and the candle on the table stays unlit. The embroiderers of Paris wrote it into their statutes that year: no work by candlelight, because work done by candle comes out worse than work done by day. For an apprentice, that meant eight years of working by daylight only.

Full story: "Your First Day as a Medieval Apprentice (Paris, 1303)" on this channel.

Source: the Paris embroiderers' statutes of 1303, as summarised in secondary sources (F. B. Millett, Craft-guilds of the thirteenth century in Paris, 1915).
