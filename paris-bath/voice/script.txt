Paris, twelve ninety-two. It is Saturday, and you are going to the baths.

Not the river. A bathhouse. This city has at least two dozen.

But nobody may cry that the water is hot until it is day. That is the law. Whoever gets up in the dark to answer that cry walks into a dark street, and the street is the danger.

So you wait for first light. Then the crier comes down your street: the baths are hot.

Two deniers buys the steam room: wooden benches, a furnace behind the wall, air you can see. Four deniers buys a tub.

Tomorrow is Sunday. On Sunday, no bathhouse in Paris may light its fire.

So today you sit in the steam, beside people from the market, and a week of smoke comes off your skin.

Medieval people washed. They just had to wait for daylight.
