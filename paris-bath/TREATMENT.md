## Facts (Livre des métiers, bathhouse-keepers' statutes, c. 1268; Paris tax roll 1292)
- 2 deniers to steam, 4 deniers to bathe.
- No one may cry their baths until it is day, because of the dangers to those who rise at the cry.
- No baths heated on Sunday or on a feast day the city keeps.
- At least two dozen bathhouses in Paris around 1292 (secondary sources say 26; script says "at least two dozen").

## Light idea
The film is a dawn. The one light the story turns on is the **bathhouse furnace**: dark street, cold blue
pre-dawn, then the sky warms, the crier walks, and the bathhouse windows and chimney come alive. Inside,
amber furnace light through a grate and steam you can see. Sunday is the same street with every chimney cold.
Ends in full daylight: "they just had to wait for daylight".
